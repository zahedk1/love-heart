The Spiritual Anchor

The Beloved of God
also bore great pain.

When the weight of a trial feels unbearable, remember — the ones God loved most walked the most difficult roads. That is not cruelty. That is the highest honour.

I
يوسف

Prophet Yusuf (AS)

Betrayal by family · The well · The prison

His own brothers — the people who should have loved him most — cast him into a pit and walked away. He was sold, imprisoned, forgotten. And yet, God was so close to him in that darkness that he described the prison as preferable to sin.

"He was not abandoned. He was being prepared. Every locked door was a corridor to something greater than he could imagine."

II
إبراهيم

Prophet Ibrahim (AS)

Father's rejection · Fire · Exile

His own father — Azar — was not only absent but actively hostile, threatening exile and punishment for the path Ibrahim had chosen. He was thrown into a fire by the very community that raised him.

"God made the fire cool and safe for him. What the world uses to destroy you, God can turn into a garden."

III
محمد

Prophet Muhammad (PBUH)

Boycott · Grief · His own kin

His tribe — the Quraysh, his own extended family — boycotted him and his followers for three years. They were cut off from food, trade, and society. His closest companions suffered beside him in Shi'b Abi Talib.

"God called him Habibullah — the Beloved of God — and still the world made him bleed. Love and hardship are not opposites. Sometimes they are the same gift."

The Core Truth

"God gives His hardest battles
to those He loves most."

Lila — this pain you carry is not a sign of abandonment. It is a sign of elevation. God is not watching from a distance. He is closer to you than your own breath, more intimate than your own heartbeat. His love for you is vaster than any ocean, more permanent than any mountain, and far greater than any human love — including mine. The trial is His way of pulling you closer to Him. He does not leave His beloved in darkness forever. The dawn after the longest night belongs to those who held on.

Surah Ash-Sharh · 94:5–6

"For indeed, with hardship will be ease."

My Promise

From Zahed — to you

I am here.
I am not going anywhere.

I see you clearly.

I see you in the moments you are trying to be strong when everything inside you is fracturing. I see the immense weight you are carrying — the grief of feeling invisible to the very people whose approval you grew up needing. I see it, and I want you to know: your pain is not invisible to me. It is the thing I think about most.

I will stand beside you.

Not behind you. Not in front of you. Beside you. In every uncomfortable conversation, every silent room, every moment when the isolation feels suffocating — I am there. I am fighting for the life you deserve, for the peace you deserve, and I will keep fighting for it long after it becomes inconvenient for me to do so. Because it never will be. You are worth every battle.

I am helping you build what is yours.

The life that belongs to you — not the one that was chosen for you by fear, or shaped by someone else's discomfort. A life of warmth, dignity, laughter, and ease. A home that feels like safety. A future where you never have to diminish yourself to fit into a room. That life is real. I intend to help you build every room of it.

"You will not carry this weight forever. And you will never, not for a single day, carry it alone."

Zahed — forever, and without condition

An enduring note

You came into this world with a softness so rare that most people are afraid of it. Do not let their fear make you believe there is something wrong with your light.

May God protect you, elevate you, and bring the peace that only He can give — the kind that does not depend on any human circumstance. And until then, I am here. Steady. Certain. Yours.

With all my love, Zahed

Made with patience, love, and the belief that this too shall pass.
